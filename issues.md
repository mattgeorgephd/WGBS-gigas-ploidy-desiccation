## Design and statistics

### I-01 (S1) Heat treatment is completely confounded with experiment and sequencing batch in the WGBS data

**Where.** Methods 2 and 2.2; every result that uses the heat contrast: heat-DML, "interaction-DML", Figure 1B, Figure 2, Figure 3, Figures 5 to 9, Table 1, Discussion 4.2 to 4.9.

**Evidence (verified).**
- `metadata.xlsx`, sheet `list`: the 10 heat libraries are all Zymo project zr3534 (`dataset = ronit`); the 12 control libraries are all Zymo project zr3644 (`dataset = yaamini`). `code/2_WGBS_Methylkit-all-gigas.R:149-150` builds the heat covariate as `rep("yes", 10), rep("no", 12)` in that file order. The author has confirmed that the controls are animals from a separate experiment.
- The two sets were sequenced as different projects and differ systematically as libraries (Bismark summary of the June 2021 alignment, `07_documentation/upstream_processing/061021-big_bismark_summary_report.txt`):

  | | Heat (zr3534, n = 10) | Control (zr3644, n = 12) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Read pairs, mean (range) | 77.2 M (66.1 to 90.6 M) | 49.6 M (36.2 to 58.1 M) |
  | Duplicate alignments, mean (range) | 18.3% (16.5 to 20.6%) | 11.6% (10.2 to 12.6%) |
  | CpGs with at least 10x per library | 4.41 to 5.51 M | 1.98 to 4.00 M |
  | Non-CpG methylation (CHH), mean | 0.51% | 0.50% |

  Read depth, duplication rate, and per-library 10x coverage do not overlap between the two sets. Non-CpG methylation is equal, so bisulfite conversion was comparable.
- The control libraries are not the heat experiment's own controls. The sample guide in `README.md` lists control animals D01 to D08 and T01 to T08 for the heat experiment; none was sequenced (`gene_expression/data/zr3534_wgbs_info.csv` lists only heat-treated animals).

**Why it matters.** When a batch variable is perfectly aligned with the variable of interest, no statistical adjustment can separate them (Leek et al. 2010, Nat Rev Genet 11:733, doi:10.1038/nrg2825). Every difference between the two experiments is an equally valid explanation for a "heat-DML": source population and family, age, season, holding conditions, tissue handling, DNA extraction, library batch, flow cell, and depth. The 2,351 heat-DML, the overlap set built from them, and the percent CpG methylation interaction in Figure 1B (F(1,18) = 14.49) cannot be attributed to heat on the present evidence. The interaction in Figure 1B is, numerically, opposite-sign ploidy differences in the two experiments (heat set: diploid minus triploid = +0.86 points; control set: -0.65 points).

**What is not affected.** The ELISA (N = 32) used the heat experiment's own control animals on one plate, so its heat effect is free of this confound (see I-10 and I-12 for its own caveats). The ploidy contrast is balanced across the two experiments (5 + 6 diploid against 5 + 6 triploid), so batch does not align with ploidy.

**Options.**
1. Sequence the heat experiment's own controls (D01 to D08, T01 to T08 ctenidia), if tissue or DNA remains. This is the only route to an unconfounded heat contrast.
2. Keep the data and reframe: report the contrast as "heat-treated cohort versus control cohort", state the confound in Methods and in the limitations, and remove mechanistic interpretation of heat-DML.
3. Shift the weight of the paper to what the design supports: the ploidy contrast, tested within each cohort and reported where it replicates in both, and the ELISA for the heat response.

The pipeline will produce the within-cohort ploidy analyses and the replication table under any of these options.

**Needs from authors.** Whether option 1 is possible; otherwise which framing. Also the provenance of the control animals (I-36).

### I-02 (S1) "Interaction-DML" are the overlap of two main-effect lists, not a tested interaction

**Where.** Methods 2.5; Results 3.2, 3.3, 3.5; Figures 2G, 3A, 4, 8, 9; Table 1; Discussion 4.3 and Conclusions.

**Evidence (verified).** `code/2_WGBS_Methylkit-all-gigas.R:400-413` builds the set by merging the ploidy-DML and heat-DML tables on their row names, then takes the rows and the `meth.diff` values from the heat table. Recomputed from `tables/DML-getMethylDiff-*.csv`: the 200 "interaction" loci are exactly the intersection of the 1,038 ploidy-DML and the 2,351 heat-DML, and their `meth.diff` column equals the heat contrast. The three classes in the text are sign combinations of the two main effects: 28 with both positive, 49 with both negative, 123 with opposite signs.

**Why it matters.** A locus that differs by ploidy and also differs by heat shows two additive effects. It says nothing about whether the response to heat differs between ploidies, which is what the manuscript claims ("methylation responses to heat differed between diploids and triploids"; "thermal exposure can actively reverse baseline ploidy-associated methylation differences"; "contrasting methylation patterns between diploids and triploids following heat exposure"). "Opposite sign" here means only that triploids are higher than diploids while the heat cohort is lower than the control cohort, or the reverse. Inferring an interaction from two separate significance calls is a known error (Gelman and Stern 2006, Am Stat 60:328, doi:10.1198/000313006X152649; Nieuwenhuis et al. 2011, Nat Neurosci 14:1105, doi:10.1038/nn.2886).

**Options.**
1. Rename the set to what it is (for example "loci shared by the ploidy and heat contrasts") and rewrite the interpretation.
2. Fit a model with a ploidy by heat term. In methylKit this can be done by using the product of the two indicators as the tested variable with both main effects as covariates; DSS fits the general design directly (Park and Wu 2016, Bioinformatics 32:1446, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btw026).
3. Both: option 1 as description, option 2 as the test.

Under I-01, a formally tested interaction is still ploidy by cohort, and the text must say so.

**Needs from authors.** Choice among the options. The pipeline will implement the legacy overlap set (to reproduce the manuscript numbers) and the formal test.

**Update 2026-11-19 (verified).** Step 05 regenerates the set in both modes: 200 loci without the upper coverage filter (28, 49, 123) and 159 with it (27, 49, 83); see I-05 and I-09. Because the legacy table carries the heat contrast's values, every "methylation difference" quoted for an interaction-DML in Table 1, Figure 9 and Results 3.5 is heat cohort minus control cohort at that locus, for all animals; for example the "+21%" of nesprin-1 and the "-31%" of the CAD protein. None of these numbers describes a difference between ploidies in the response to heat.

**Verification 2026-11-19.** The 20 values of Table 1 were recomputed from the 22 coverage files as read-pooled differences over all 22 libraries: triploid minus diploid for the ploidy rows, heat cohort minus control cohort for the heat rows and for the interaction rows. All 20 agree within 0.005 points. Across all 200 shared loci the archived interaction table equals the heat list (largest difference 5e-9) and never the ploidy value. `06_diagnostics/table1_group_means.R` regenerates them from the step 05 table, together with the values of each of the four groups and of each library (`table1_group_means.csv`, `table1_library_values.csv`); the numbers below are from those files. What follows for the text:

- The three interaction values of Table 1 describe neither ploidy. Heat cohort minus control cohort within each ploidy, in percentage points, read-pooled over the 5 or 6 libraries of each group (descriptive; no test):

  | Locus (value in Table 1) | Diploids | Triploids |
  |---|---|---|
  | CAD protein, NC_047564.1:8,425,110 (-31) | -54.5 | -8.4 |
  | Nesprin-1, NC_047561.1:28,607,016 (+21) | -0.8 | +40.2 |
  | Dihydropyrimidinase, NW_022994979.1:42,498 (+31) | +24.1 | +35.0 |

- At the first two loci one of the four groups stands apart (diploid heat for CAD, triploid heat for nesprin-1). An overlap of two pooled contrasts selects for exactly this, so "two additive effects", as written above, is not an exact description of every shared locus. Whether a locus shows a ploidy-specific difference between the cohorts is a question that only a model with an interaction term answers (option 2; analysis A2 of `06_diagnostics/dml_sensitivity.R`, see I-06).
- The group that stands apart does so through few animals. At the CAD locus three of the five diploid heat libraries are at 3%, 12% and 33% methylation and the other 19 libraries are between 67% and 100%. Nesprin-1 is bimodal among animals in every group (per-library values 0% to 86%). Genotype (I-08) and cohort (I-01) explain such a pattern as well as a ploidy-specific response to heat does.
- "Hypermethylated" has two meanings in the draft for this set: both contrasts positive in Results 3.2 and Figure 4 (28 loci), and heat contrast positive in Figure 9 and Results 3.5 (106 loci). Eleven of the 27 entries behind the interaction counts of Figure 9 are loci of opposite sign.
- Statements about individual loci that the draft's own tables contradict (nesprin-1 and dihydropyrimidinase as "contrasting"; LAP as an interaction locus) are collected in I-45.

### I-03 (S1) GO enrichment treats every transcript isoform as an independent gene

**Where.** Methods 2.6; Results 3.4 and 3.5; Figures 5 to 9; Table 1; most of Discussion 4.1 to 4.3 and 4.6 to 4.9.

**Evidence (verified).**
- `code/5_WGBS_Functional-enrichement-analysis-BOTH.R:186-206`: the topGO universe is the list of transcript IDs in `geneid2go-union1x-BOTH.tab`, and a transcript is flagged as "of interest" if its gene contains a DML. Every isoform of a gene with one DML is therefore counted as a separate significant gene carrying the same GO terms.
- The RefSeq annotation has 63,341 mRNAs for 31,371 protein-coding genes; 613 genes have 10 or more isoforms.
- The top terms are single genes repeated. Recomputed from `GOterms/BOTH-*/all-*-EnrichedGO-DML-withTranscript.csv` (transcripts annotated to each top term) and the DML annotation tables on gannet (`analyses/GOterm-annotation/BOTH/*.GOAnnot`):

  | DML set, ontology | Example top term | Annotated / significant transcripts | Genes behind the significant transcripts |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Interaction, BP | pyrimidine nucleobase catabolic process, p = 1.8e-21 | 12 / 12 | 1: dihydropyrimidinase (12 isoforms, one DML) |
  | Interaction, BP | nuclear matrix organization, p = 1.8e-21 | 12 / 12 | 1: nesprin-1 (12 isoforms) |
  | Heat, BP | iron ion import across plasma membrane, p < 1e-30 | 73 / 51 | 2: LAP (50 isoforms) and LOC117680672 |
  | Ploidy, BP | gephyrin clustering involved in postsynaptic density assembly, p = 4.3e-19 | 13 / 13 | 1: neurexin-1 (13 isoforms) |

  Across the three DML sets, the ten top BP terms rest on DML in 7 genes (ploidy), 4 genes (heat), and 3 genes (interaction). Of all 90 top-ten terms (three sets, three ontologies), 70 rest on two or fewer genes and 43 on exactly one.
- No multiple testing correction: 12,720 BP terms were tested per DML set with a raw p < 0.01 cutoff (`:1009-1063`), using the classic Fisher test that ignores the GO hierarchy (`:255`).
- The background does not match the Methods. Methods: genes with CpGs at 10x in all samples. Code: transcripts overlapping a CpG with at least 1x in any sample (`code/4_WGBS_GOterm-Annotation-BOTH.ipynb`, cells 55 to 74), which is nearly every annotated transcript.
- Annotation bug: cell 10 of the same notebook rebuilds transcript IDs with `sed 's/^/XM_/'`, which relabels the 157 `NM_` (curated RefSeq) and 8,059 `XR_` queries in the blastx file as `XM_`; the `NM_` transcripts then fail to join and lose their GO terms.

**Why it matters.** The reported p-values (1e-17 to below 1e-30) measure how many isoforms a few genes have, not functional enrichment. The gene lists in Table 1 and Figure 9 are therefore lists of isoform-rich genes that contain at least one DML, and the functional narrative built on them (DNA replication and meiosis for ploidy; MAPK, ER overload, RNA processing for heat; tRNA modification and "alarm system" thresholds for interaction; AK9, CAD, nesprin-1, LAP, dihydropyrimidinase as mechanisms) has no statistical support from this analysis. Two further biases compound it: gene-set tests on methylation data are biased toward genes with many assayed CpGs unless that is modelled (Geeleher et al. 2013, Bioinformatics 29:1851, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btt311; Maksimovic et al. 2021, Genome Biol 22:173, doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02388-x), and a background that includes genes that could never be detected inflates enrichment (Timmons et al. 2015, Genome Biol 16:186, doi:10.1186/s13059-015-0761-7).

**Proposed resolution.** Test at gene level (one entry per gene, GO terms pooled over its transcripts); use as background only genes containing at least one of the CpGs that were tested; account for the number of tested CpGs per gene; control the false discovery rate; use a hierarchy-aware algorithm (Alexa et al. 2006, Bioinformatics 22:1600, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btl140). The legacy analysis will be reproduced unchanged alongside it so the two can be compared. It is likely that few or no terms survive; the manuscript should be prepared for that outcome.

**Needs from authors.** Agreement to make the gene-level analysis primary.

### I-05 (S2) The 99.9th percentile coverage filter described in the Methods was never applied

**Where.** Methods 2.5 ("removing CpG in the 99.9th percentile of coverage"); the CpG set behind every WGBS result.

### I-06 (S2) No overdispersion correction in the differential methylation model

**Where.** Methods 2.5; all DML.

**Evidence (verified).** `code/2_WGBS_Methylkit-all-gigas.R:154-162` calls `calculateDiffMeth()` with a covariate and all other arguments at their defaults, which means `overdispersion = "none"`: a binomial logistic regression in which reads from different animals are treated as exchangeable draws.

**Why it matters.** With biological replicates, variation among animals exceeds binomial sampling variation, and a model without overdispersion is anticonservative. The methylKit authors' own benchmark shows a markedly higher false positive rate without the correction and recommends `overdispersion = "MN"` with a chi-squared test (Wreczycka et al. 2017, J Biotechnol 261:105, doi:10.1016/j.jbiotec.2017.08.007). The 20% difference threshold limits but does not remove the problem.

**Proposed resolution.** Report a sensitivity analysis with the correction; if the authors agree, make it primary.

**Needs from authors.** Whether to change the primary model.

### I-08 (S2) Sequence variants were not masked

**Where.** Methods 2.4 and 2.5.

**Evidence.** No step in the repository identifies or removes C/T polymorphisms. The Methods cite Venkataraman et al. (2022) for methodology; that project included a C/T SNP identification step with BS-Snper (verified: its OSF archive, doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/YGCTB, contains `code/07-BS-SNPer.ipynb`), which has no counterpart here. Diploid and triploid oysters in each cohort come from different crosses, and the two cohorts are different stocks (inference from the design; to be confirmed by the authors).

**Why it matters.** In bisulfite data a C-to-T variant is indistinguishable from an unmethylated cytosine (Liu et al. 2012, Genome Biol 13:R61, doi:10.1186/gb-2012-13-7-r61). Genetic differences between stocks can therefore appear as DML with large effect sizes, which is the class the 20% threshold selects for. In triploids allele dosage adds intermediate values.

**Proposed resolution.** Flag DML whose per-animal methylation values sit at genotype-like levels; if BAM files are still available (gannet `/panopea/061021-big/`), call variants and mask them. To be scoped after the legacy reproduction.

### I-10 (S2) ELISA conclusions for ploidy and interaction depend on the transformation

**Where.** Results 3.1 (5-mC statistics); Figure 1A letters; Discussion 4.5.

**Evidence (verified in R).** `code/5-mC & 5mCpG plots.Rmd:84-101` applies an arcsine square-root transformation when a Shapiro-Wilk test on the pooled raw values is significant. Normality is an assumption about model residuals, not about the pooled response; the residuals of the untransformed model pass (p = 0.59).

| Scale | Ploidy | Heat | Ploidy x heat | Residual normality | Equal variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Untransformed | p = 0.033 | p = 0.0014 | p = 0.036 | p = 0.59 | p = 0.043 |
| Arcsine square root (manuscript) | p = 0.057 | p = 0.0009 | p = 0.058 | p = 0.83 | p = 0.33 |
| Log | p = 0.12 | p = 0.0008 | p = 0.11 | p = 0.78 | p = 0.65 |

Residual normality is Shapiro-Wilk on model residuals; equal variance is Brown-Forsythe across the four groups. Three versions of this analysis exist in the repository (untransformed, Johnson, arcsine), and an older figure prints the untransformed p-values.

**Why it matters.** The heat effect on global 5-mC is robust. The ploidy effect and the interaction are not: they are significant only on the untransformed scale, which is the one that fails the equal-variance check, and are not significant on either scale that meets both model assumptions (arcsine 0.057 and 0.058; log 0.12 and 0.11). The arcsine analysis in the manuscript is therefore defensible, although it was selected by a test on the wrong quantity. The statement that diploids increased while triploids "showed no significant change" compares a significant with a non-significant result, which does not establish a difference (Gelman and Stern 2006); it needs the interaction, and the interaction is the fragile term. The Tukey letters are computed on the transformed scale and the reported means are not back-transformed.

**Proposed resolution.** Choose the scale from diagnostics of the fitted model, state the choice and the reason in the Methods, report the sensitivity to scale, and report effect sizes with confidence intervals on the original scale. Rewrite Discussion 4.5 so that it does not rest on the interaction.

**Needs from authors.** Agreement on the scale; see also I-12, which could change the values themselves.

### I-41 (S1) Location and function "enrichment" of DML reflects where methylation and between-animal variation sit in the genome

**Where.** Results 3.3 ("strongly biased toward gene-associated sequences, with substantial enrichment in introns and coding sequences"), 3.4, 3.5; Figure 3A; Discussion sections that interpret location or function of DML.

**The argument in brief.** The draft compares DML with all analysed CpGs (location) and with all annotated transcripts (function). Three successively stricter comparisons were made here.
1. Against all tested CpGs, DML look strongly enriched in genes and in housekeeping functions. So does any set of methylated CpGs.
2. Against the CpGs with a mean methylation between 10% and 90%, the range that holds all but 2 of the DML ("eligible" CpGs), the gene-body excess shrinks from an odds ratio of about 7 to about 2.5, and the functional enrichment disappears.
3. Against CpGs matched for methylation level and for variation among animals of the same group, or against CpGs that pass the same 20-point rule when the ploidy labels are shuffled, nothing remains of the gene-body excess.

The first version of this issue stopped at step 2 and presented the remaining twofold gene-body excess as a property of the DML, and it stated that no GO term is enriched without the sensitivity of that statement. Independent verification showed both to be overstated; this version replaces them (see the corrections table).

**Evidence (verified).**
- *Eligible CpGs.* A DML is a CpG whose read-pooled methylation differs by more than 20 percentage points between two groups. In practice that confines DML to CpGs with a mean methylation across libraries between 10% and 90%: all but 2 of the 3,189 distinct ploidy and heat DML (3,389 list entries) lie in that range, and all but 1 in corrected mode. Of the 480,579 tested CpGs, 84.6% have a mean below 10% and 1.8% a mean above 90%; **65,480 (13.6%)** lie between. The range is an empirical screen, not a mathematical requirement. **Why it matters.** The location and function results in the draft are presented as effects of ploidy and heat. What they show is where methylation sits in the oyster genome and where animals differ from one another. Compared fairly, DML show no preference for gene bodies beyond that of other variably methylated CpGs and no established functional category; one group of related terms (vesicle coat, for the cohort contrast) is unresolved. The small excess in coding sequence that remains after matching (odds ratio 1.25 and 1.17) is also shown by CpG lists made with shuffled ploidy labels (median 1.18 after the same matching) and cannot be attributed to ploidy; for heat it cannot be tested. That removes the basis for the enrichment statements of Results 3.3, for Results 3.4 and 3.5, Figures 5 to 9, Table 1, and for the mechanistic passages of the Discussion (DNA replication and meiosis for ploidy; MAPK, ER overload and RNA processing for heat; tRNA modification and "alarm system" thresholds for the interaction). None of the terms those passages rest on is among the near-misses. The clathrin and vesicle-coat family of the heat CC list is the only part of the draft's functional results that a gene-level analysis leaves open. The descriptive statement that survives is that DML lie in constitutively methylated, broadly expressed genes; it is equally true of CpG lists made with shuffled labels and so says nothing about ploidy or heat.

A further result from the same permutations bears on every ploidy finding and is taken up in I-06: with ploidy labels shuffled within cohort and acute-heat-shock stratum (300 permutations), a median of 762 CpGs pass the DML rule (5th to 95th percentile 510 to 1,226), against 1,038 observed, and 13% of the permutations reach 1,038. Repeats with 1,000 to 3,000 permutations give medians of 739 to 753, a 95th percentile of 1,388 to 1,491, and 13% to 16% at or above 1,038; with labels shuffled within cohort only, the sensitivity script gives a median of 749 and 11% (113 of 1,000).

**Limits of this conclusion.**
1. Absence of enrichment is not evidence that the DML are functionally irrelevant. The analysis can exclude only strong enrichment of small categories (about 2.4-fold for heat and 3.1-fold for ploidy for a term of 20 to 49 genes) and moderate enrichment (1.2- to 1.6-fold) of categories above 200 genes.
2. For the heat contrast no label-permutation null exists, because heat is confounded with cohort (I-01). Calibration for the heat list rests on uniform and propensity-weighted draws and on permutations of the ploidy labels.
3. Eligibility and the number of eligible CpGs per gene are computed from the same 22 libraries as the contrast. For 16% of DML one group mean lies outside 10% to 90%, and 7% to 8% of DML genes have no eligible CpG other than their DML. By the verifying agent's argument, which was reasoned and not tested by simulation, the resulting bias favours finding categories that gained methylation and does not hide them. The number of eligible CpGs is also an incomplete measure of a gene's opportunity to hold a DML: a bias variable built from each CpG's chance of passing the rule (mean, within-group standard deviation, coverage) removes every near-miss (smallest q 0.44).
4. The annotation is old (I-42) and covers 65% of protein-coding genes (20,279 of 31,371). 830 of the 14,170 GO IDs of the frozen map are not in GO.db 3.18.0 and are dropped; 395 of them are secondary IDs of current terms and could be remapped (435 are obsolete). With them remapped the CC universe grows from 5,738 to 5,976 genes, and the exact stratified test calls six heat CC terms instead of one: "organelle" and three of its child terms, "membrane coat" and "coated membrane". The ploidy result, and heat BP and MF, do not change.
5. The count of terms depends on the settings, for the broadest terms and the vesicle-coat group only. With the range widened to 5% to 95%, 8 heat CC terms pass in step 10's own test; with terms of at least 10 or 20 genes the exact stratified test calls 5 or 10 heat CC terms (the organelle family, "protein-containing complex" and the vesicle-coat group); with a minimum of 100 genes 10 ploidy BP terms pass in legacy mode (none in corrected mode, smallest q 0.063), at a setting where label permutations give a term in 9% to 16% of runs and 10 or more BP terms in 6.0%. A categorical "none" or "one" overstates what the data can decide.
6. DML are assigned to gene bodies only; promoters are not considered. Gene status is binary: the number, size and clustering of DML in a gene are ignored.
7. The false discovery rate is applied per DML set and ontology (nine families per mode). The chance of at least one hit somewhere under the null is about three times the per-family rate; adjusted across the three ontologies together, the smallest heat q under the exact test is 0.13.
8. No hierarchy-aware algorithm is used (I-03).
9. The heat contrast remains a cohort contrast (I-01) whatever the enrichment method.

**Options.**
1. *Location.* Report where DML lie descriptively (94% in gene bodies, like other variably methylated CpGs) and remove the statements of enrichment in introns and coding sequence. If a test of location is wanted, use the label-permutation distribution as the background for ploidy, or a background matched on methylation level and between-animal variation; neither supports a gene-body preference. The comparison with all analysed CpGs (Figure 3A, first bar) should not be presented as a test.
2. *Function.* State that no GO category is established as over-represented among genes holding DML, with the power limits above and the one unresolved group named; replace Figures 5 to 9 and Table 1 with a supplementary table of genes holding DML, labelled as descriptive.
3. Keep a functional analysis but change its question, for example enrichment among genes ranked by a region-level statistic within one cohort (ploidy within controls, ploidy within heat), which also avoids I-01. This is new analysis, not a correction, and I-06 has to be settled first, because it decides which loci count as different at all.
4. Refresh the annotation first (I-42) and repeat option 2. It is unlikely to change the outcome, because the null result comes from the choice of universe and the adjustment for CpG number, not from annotation coverage; this has not been tested.

**Needs from authors.** Choice among the options. Options 1 and 2 are what the current data support.

**Status of the pipeline.** Step 10 implements the Wallenius analysis only. To be added (plan, phase 5): an exact test beside the Wallenius value; the logarithm of the CpG count as the bias variable (goseq's spline fits poorly on the raw count, where 94% of genes lie below its second knot; the result does not change); remapping of secondary GO IDs; the expectation adjusted for CpG count beside the unadjusted `expected` and `enrichment` columns of `go_gene_level_<set>_<mode>.csv`, which are topGO's Fisher values (for "organelle" the table shows 914.4 where the adjusted expectation is 904; for heat MF "protein binding" 364.7 against 392.7); a label-permutation control and a spike-in positive control in `06_diagnostics/`; the matched and permuted location comparisons as a diagnostic that writes the second table above. Until those exist, the numbers of that table and of the calibration and power paragraphs come from the verification record (`07_documentation/agent_workflows/verification_scripts/C4a-eligible-theory`, `C4b-eligible-reimplementation`, `C5-feature-odds`), where two agents working separately obtained the same matched odds ratios (1.13 and 1.01).

### I-46 (S2) qPCR: actin, the only reference gene, does not behave as a stable reference

**Where.** Every relative expression of the legacy qPCR analysis; Discussion 4.5 and 4.7 (HSP90).

**Evidence (step 12; `qpcr_reference_gene.csv`, `qpcr_normalization_check.csv`).**
- The actin Cq spans 21.9 to 35.3 cycles across the 32 animals. Group means are 26.7 (diploid control), 26.4 (diploid heat), 24.2 (triploid control) and 25.5 (triploid heat). It differs between ploidies by -1.68 cycles (95% interval -3.57 to 0.20; p = 0.078), or -1.07 cycles (p = 0.14) without one failed sample; and, without that sample, between heat and control by 1.14 cycles (-0.32 to 2.60; p = 0.12). A difference of one cycle in the reference is a twofold difference in every normalized target.
- Within groups a target's Cq rises by less than one cycle per cycle of actin: 0.68 for HSC70 (0.56 to 0.80), 0.59 for HATHaP2, 0.54 for SOD, 0.83 for HSP90 (0.57 to 1.10), 0.88 for ATP synthetase. Subtracting the actin Cq therefore does not remove the dependence on template amount, and animals with a late actin Cq read as high expressers.
- For HSP90, heat against control is 2.17-fold as delta Cq, 1.90-fold (p = 0.077) with the actin Cq as covariate, and 0.99-fold (p = 0.98) in raw Cq. Under delta Cq seven of the nine genes read as higher in heat animals.

**Why it matters.** Normalization to a single reference gene needs evidence that the gene is stable under the conditions compared (Bustin et al. 2009, Clin Chem 55:611, doi:10.1373/clinchem.2008.112797, the MIQE guidelines). Every relative expression of the legacy analysis rests on actin, and the one qPCR effect with p < 0.05 in step 12 (HSP90 and heat) is carried by the difference in actin between heat and control animals.

**Needs from authors.** Whether a second reference gene was run; RNA input and quality per sample.

### I-47 (S2) qPCR: most targets were measured at or below their limit of detection, and substituting Cq 45 for non-detects creates values

**Where.** The legacy qPCR scripts (`08_archive/gene_expression/scripts/`, `08_archive/ELISA/`); the HSP90 ploidy statement of the Discussion.

**Evidence (step 12; `qpcr_nondetects.csv`, `qpcr_sensitivity.csv`, `qpcr_models.csv`).**
- Non-detect wells among the 64 wells of the 32 animals: HSC70 0, HATHaP2 2, HSP90 3, DNMT1 10 (16%), HAT 14 (22%), MBD2 19 (30%), HIF1A 20 (31%), MeCP2 49 (77%). Taking the limit of detection as the level at which 95% of wells are detected (Bustin et al. 2009, cited in I-46), only HSC70, HATHaP2 and HSP90 of the eight targets with replicate-level data are quantifiable. MeCP2 has no group with half its wells detected. For HAT, MBD2 and HIF1A six to eight animals have one detected well of two, and the result depends on how that well is read (MBD2, ploidy: 0.91-fold or 1.53-fold; HIF1A, interaction: 0.52-fold or 1.06-fold).
- No detected Cq is later than 39.94, so the runs appear to have had 40 cycles and no well can have a Cq of 45. Some legacy scripts substitute 45 for a non-detect and then subtract the actin Cq. For the failed sample D_N_N05 (actin Cq 35.3; six of its eight targets have no detected well) this produces the highest "expression" of DNMT1 and HAT in the data set and the lowest of HSP90, and it moves the legacy p-value of HSP90 by ploidy from 0.068 to 0.035. For MBD2 the heat effect has p = 0.009 with non-detects dropped, 0.67 with 45 substituted, and 0.090 in a censored regression. Both legacy treatments of non-detects are biased (McCall et al. 2014, Bioinformatics 30:2310, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btu239).

**Resolution.** Step 12 reproduces both legacy variants and reports beside them a censored regression, in which a non-detect is a value known only to lie beyond the last cycle. The test of that regression was checked by simulation inside the step: at a nominal 5% it rejects a true null in 4.5% to 6.0% of simulated data sets, against 6.8% to 9.6% without the small-sample correction (`qpcr_censored_test_size.csv`).

**Needs from authors.** If qPCR stays in the paper: agreement not to report DNMT1, MBD2, MeCP2, HIF1A or HAT as quantitative results, and the number of cycles of the runs.
