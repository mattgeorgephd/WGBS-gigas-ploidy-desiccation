# 06_diagnostics

Checks that the pipeline reproduces what it should, and controls for the analyses that replace legacy ones. These scripts are not part of the analysis: no analysis step reads their outputs. Step 13, which ties the manuscript's numbers to outputs, may quote them.

Or through the pipeline, as option (h), which the paper did not adopt: `Rscript run_pipeline.R --diagnostics` runs every script, `--diagnostics cohort_separation,table1_group_means` the scripts named, in the order of [`diagnostics.csv`](diagnostics.csv) (one row per script: order, the analyses it reads, whether it needs the network, minutes and memory, whether a value of the paper's manuscript map reads its table, and the files of `04_output/not_adopted/corrected/` it reads), after step 11 and before step 13, whatever `--steps` selects. A script whose needs are not met (the network, or the stamp and every file of the analysis `corrected` listed in column `reads_corrected_files`) is reported as "not run", with the files that are absent, and the run goes on.

Seven scripts (`locus_nc047563`, `coverage_filter_variants`, `check_methylkit_equivalence`, `feature_odds_matched`, `dml_sensitivity`, `dml_corrected_test`, `library_methylation_level`) read the per-CpG tables of step 05 of the analysis `corrected` (`united_cpg_corrected.csv.gz`, and most of them `diffmeth_{ploidy,heat}_corrected.csv.gz`). Those tables stay tracked in git, as an exception: the per-CpG tables of every other optional analysis are git-ignored since 2026-11-20 (`04_output/not_adopted/README.md`, "What is tracked"). The diagnostics write only into this folder; `tests/snapshot_paper.R` checks that `fastq_read_names.csv` and `table1_group_means.csv`, which 15 values of the paper's manuscript map read, do not change.

Random CpGs are "enriched" for dozens to hundreds of terms against the tested universe (15 to 200 terms per draw and ontology) and for none under the third analysis. Under the second analysis the number varies widely (medians from 1.5 to 87.5; 0 to 194 terms per draw and ontology). Enrichment under the first two therefore reflects which genes are methylated and how many methylated CpGs they hold, not ploidy or heat. See issue I-41.

**Limits of this control.** CpGs drawn uniformly from the eligible CpGs are the one null under which the number of eligible CpGs per gene is exactly the right adjustment, so this control is easy on the third analysis and cannot tell a calibrated test from a conservative one. Independent verification found the Wallenius analysis conservative under this null (a term in under 1% of runs), at the nominal level when ploidy labels are shuffled within cohort (a term in 4% to 6% of 250 permutations), and above it (6% to 17%) for heat-sized lists drawn in proportion to each CpG's chance of passing the 20-point rule. A label-permutation control and a spike-in positive control would complete it; neither is built without the author's approval (scope decision of 2026-11-19). The figures quoted come from `07_documentation/agent_workflows/verification_scripts/C4a-eligible-theory`.

## Sensitivity of the DML calls

`dml_sensitivity.R` shows how the DML lists of step 05 change under each defensible alternative to the analysis of the draft, so that the authors can decide what the paper should report. Each analysis is one entry of a registry in the script and one `methylKit::calculateDiffMeth` call on the CpGs of `united_cpg_<mode>.csv.gz`. All analyses therefore test the same CpGs, and a DML is defined as in step 05 (`config$dml_qvalue`, `config$dml_difference`).

| Analysis | What it changes | Issues |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | Nothing: ploidy with heat as covariate and heat with ploidy as covariate, as in step 05. It is fitted first, and the script stops unless it reproduces the p-values, q-values, both DML lists, and the loci in both lists of step 05 | |
| A1 | Overdispersion correction (`overdispersion = "MN"`, `test = "Chisq"`, the setting of Venkataraman et al. 2022) | I-06 |
| A2 | A tested ploidy by heat interaction, without and with the correction, set against the loci in both primary lists. Because heat is confounded with cohort, it is a ploidy by cohort interaction | I-02, I-01 |
| A3 | Both contrasts without the heat libraries that also had the acute heat shock | I-04 |
| A4 | Ploidy among the controls alone and among the heat-treated libraries alone, without and with the correction | I-07, I-01 |
| A5 | Heat against control among diploids alone and among triploids alone. Still a contrast between cohorts | I-01 |

All analyses run in `legacy` mode. A0, A1 and A2 also run in `corrected` mode (I-05).

### How to read the size of a list

A long list is not by itself evidence of differences between the groups, and a short list is not evidence of their absence. Read each row of `dml_counts.csv` with the following.

1. **`n_effect_above_cutoff` against `n_dml`.** Where the two are equal, the q-value criterion removes nothing: the list is the set of CpGs whose pooled group difference exceeds the cutoff. The difference between two small groups is noisier than the difference between two large ones, so that set is larger by chance in the analyses on fewer libraries (A3, A4, A5) and for the interaction (A2, four groups of five or six libraries).
2. **`permutation_reference.csv`.** It shows how many CpGs exceed the cutoff when the ploidy labels are permuted among the libraries of each cohort. Where at most 1,000 labellings exist, all are counted (924 for the controls, 252 for the heat-treated libraries); otherwise the observed labelling and 999 random ones. `share_of_labellings_at_least_observed` is a permutation p-value in which the observed labelling counts as one labelling (Phipson and Smyth 2010, Stat Appl Genet Mol Biol 9:Article 39, doi:10.2202/1544-6115.1585). A share that is not small means that the observed labels give no more CpGs above the cutoff than random labels do. A small share means that the diploid and the triploid libraries of the analysis differ (for the interaction: that their difference is not the same in the two cohorts). It does not say which CpGs differ, and it cannot separate ploidy from anything else that separates the diploid from the triploid libraries of a cohort, such as a preparation batch. The smallest share that can occur is 1 over the number of labellings used, and 2 over that number where all labellings are used and the two groups of a cohort have the same size, because exchanging the two labels gives the same count. The heat contrasts have no reference: every heat-treated library is of one cohort and every control of the other, so the heat label cannot be permuted (I-01).
3. **The p-values.** `share_eligible_p_below_0_05`, `_0_01`, `_0_001` are the shares of the eligible CpGs (mean methylation across libraries between half the difference cutoff and 100 minus half of it) with a p-value below 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001. Where the groups do not differ, a test that holds its level gives about these shares. In the analyses with the correction (`_mn_chisq`) the shares cannot be read against these nominal values: with relabelled ploidy labels that keep little of the real grouping (two exchanges in the heat cohort and the five libraries of I-49 split three to two, 163 of 300 relabellings) the correction gives 5.7%, 1.6% and 0.31% of p-values below 0.05, 0.01 and 0.001, an excess that is clear at p < 0.01 and p < 0.001 and marginal at p < 0.05; with two exchanges alone (234) 6.6%, 1.9% and 0.38%, with the three to two split alone (193) 5.8%, 1.6% and 0.32% (`dml_corrected_test/relabel_level_summary.csv`; the 163 from the rows of `relabel_level_pooled.csv`; `issues.md`, I-06, items 2 and 4). The average over all 300 relabellings (7.2%, 2.2% and 0.47%) is higher because it includes relabellings that carry real structure: 66 that keep or nearly keep the real heat-cohort grouping (9.4% below 0.05) and 107 that put four or five of those libraries in one group (9.6%). Read the shares against relabelled libraries. In the analyses without the correction a larger share also arises from the dispersion alone (see 6).
4. **`statistic_needed_for_a_single_call`.** A CpG whose p-value is below `p_needed_for_a_single_call` (the q-value cutoff divided by the number of CpGs tested) has a q-value below the cutoff whatever the other CpGs do; where k CpGs are called together, a p-value k times as large is enough. The column gives the chi-squared statistic at that p-value (31.4 in legacy mode, 31.3 in corrected mode); with the correction it is the deviance difference divided by the dispersion estimate that must exceed it. The correction applies only where the dispersion estimate of a CpG is above 1; where it is not, methylKit sets the estimate to 1, so the "corrected" p-value is the uncorrected one (`analyses.csv`, column `reference_distribution`; such CpGs per list in `dml_corrected_test/correction_not_applied.csv`). A corrected list is not a list with 1% false discoveries, because the corrected test does not hold its level (see 3): relabelled libraries get corrected calls too (`dml_corrected_test/relabel_corrected_lists_summary.csv`; `issues.md`, I-06, item 7).
5. **`median_effect_size_eligible` with `n_hyper` and `n_hypo`.** A median away from 0 means that one group of libraries is shifted as a whole at CpGs with intermediate methylation. Many CpGs then pass the difference cutoff in one direction, and a list that is nearly all hyper or nearly all hypo reflects that shift and not loci that stand out from the others.
6. **`dispersion_summary.csv`.** The observed row is the dispersion of the counts that are tested. The model of the draft refers its statistic to a chi-squared distribution, which assumes a dispersion of 1. The second row shows what binomial reads give after step 05 has multiplied the counts of a library by its scaling factor and rounded (about 1.18): the scaling alone makes the uncorrected test somewhat anticonservative. The third row shows that the estimator gives about 1 for binomial counts as sequenced. The test statistics of the analyses without the correction are too large by about the observed dispersion (3.7-fold at the median eligible CpG: a factor of about 1.2 from the scaling and about 3.1 from variation among libraries).

The analyses of this script are tests of single CpGs with the variation among animals either ignored (as in the draft) or estimated per CpG from 10 to 22 libraries. Methods made for few animals share information between CpGs when they estimate that variation (the two-group model of DSS: Feng, Conneely and Wu 2014, Nucleic Acids Res 42:e69, doi:10.1093/nar/gku154), or test regions (dmrseq: Korthauer et al. 2019, Biostatistics 20:367-383, doi:10.1093/biostatistics/kxy007). The model of DSS for designs with a covariate estimates the dispersion CpG by CpG, without sharing (Park and Wu 2016, Bioinformatics 32:1446-1453, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btw026). They are not run here; see I-06.

### Test mode

`WGBS_SENSITIVITY_SUBSET=<N>` runs the same code on a seeded random sample of N CpGs of each united table and writes everything to `dml_sensitivity/test_subset/`. That folder and the `cache/` folders are git-ignored. The files of a test run test the code and are not results: q-values depend on the set of CpGs tested. The variable selects test mode as soon as it is set; a value that is empty, not a whole number, or larger than a united table stops the run before any fit.

Four points for reading these tables.

1. **Thresholds and ties.** A CpG counts as above the threshold when the absolute difference exceeds 20 points by more than 1e-9. A difference of exactly 20 points is not counted; `contrast_direction.csv` reports such CpGs separately (20 for the ploidy contrast inside the heat cohort). `dml_sensitivity.R` compares floating-point values without that margin, so its counts can differ from these by a few CpGs (3,350 against 3,349 for the control cohort; medians 4,019.5 against 4,018.5 for the heat cohort). The values that the two tables share and that differ are, in legacy mode: for the control cohort the observed count (3,350 against 3,349) and the smallest count (1,726 against 1,725); for the heat cohort the smallest count (2,721 against 2,720), the 5th percentile (2,984 against 2,983) and the median (`dml_sensitivity/permutation_reference.csv` against `library_methylation_level/relabelling_reference.csv`). The observed counts differ by one CpG, NC_047563.1:63,152,425, at which 77 of 110 pooled reads are methylated in the control diploids and 72 of 80 in the control triploids (70% against 90%); methylKit evaluates this difference to 20.000000000000007. Every CpG that `dml_sensitivity.R` counts and this script does not is a difference of exactly 20 points. For the draft's own lists the wording of the Methods ("at least 20%") against the rule of the code (more than 20) matters for one CpG: NW_022994958.1:20,934 differs by exactly 20 points between the cohorts (70% against 50%, q = 0.0004) and is in neither heat list; no CpG of the ploidy contrast lies at exactly 20 points (`contrast_direction.csv`, column `n_exactly_at_threshold`). The two scripts keep their two rules (scope decision of 2026-11-19); the review recommends deciding on the pooled counts without division, which is exact and changes no number of the 13 tables here.
2. **Complete enumeration.** Where all regroupings are counted, the share at or above the observed count is an exact p-value for the hypothesis that the grouping is unrelated to methylation. It cannot fall below (groupings as extreme as the observed one) divided by (all groupings): 2 of 252 for five against five, 1 of 21 for two against five. The share in `pair_similarity.csv` is of another kind: it is one-sided (subsets whose libraries are at least as similar to each other as those of the group), the complementary subset has its own value, and the smallest share is 1 of 252 for five of ten libraries (0.00397, the value of the heat triploids) and 1 of 924 for six of twelve.
3. **Exploratory rows.** The five high libraries are found by a rule that ignores the group labels. Setting them aside is nevertheless a choice made after seeing the data, and the rows "without the high libraries" must be read as exploration. Cutting at the second largest gap instead, which sets aside only zr3534_3 and zr3644_14, gives the same reading in legacy mode: 9,178 CpGs against a median of 4,462 for the heat cohort (1 of 126 regroupings at or above, the smallest share possible) and 2,810 against 2,957.5 for the control cohort (290 of 462). The observed counts are in `leave_one_library_out.csv`; the regroupings were computed by the code review (`out/alternative_gap_cut.log` in `07_documentation/agent_workflows/verification_scripts/S3-code-review-library-level-r2/`) and are not written by the script.
4. **What an independent check found about reading these tables** (issue I-49; scripts in `07_documentation/agent_workflows/verification_scripts/S2-I49-evidence-and-alternatives/`). `pair_similarity.csv` cannot be compared between cohorts as it stands: the control libraries have fewer reads, and a correlation falls with sampling noise; at equal read depth the heat triploids are as similar to each other as ordinary control libraries are. `level_slope_deciles.csv` shows an association of sign that is in effect the contrast between the two high control libraries and the other ten, not a graded dependence on the level. `level_correlations.csv`: the correlations of the level with the first principal component and with the mean over eligible CpGs are those of the five high libraries against the other 17. `relabelling_reference.csv`: "2 of 252" for the heat cohort mostly restates that its three high libraries are all diploid; the next regrouping is 206 CpGs behind. The computations behind these statements are not written by the script; by the scope decision of 2026-11-19 it is not extended without the author's approval.
